\subsection*{Problem 1} 
Consider the following linear programming problem in standard form:
\\~\\$\begin{aligned} \text { minimize } & 2 x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3} \\ \text { subject to: } & 3 x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{4}=4 \\ & 6 x_{1}+2 x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}=5 \\ & x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}, x_{4} \geq 0 \end{aligned}$
\\~\\Consider the basis $\beta=\{3, 4\}$:
\\~\\(a) Compute the basic feasible solution, $\bfx_\beta$, corresponding to this basis.
\\(b) Is the solution optimal? Justify your answer.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Answer:} 
\\~\\\textbf{(a)} $\left[\begin{array}{cccc}{3} & {1} & {0} & {1}\\ {6} & {2} & {1} & {1}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{x_1} \\ {x_2} \\ {x_3} \\ {x_4}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{c}{4} \\ {5}\end{array}\right]$.
\\~\\With respect to basis: $\left[\begin{array}{c}{x_3} \\ {x_4}\end{array}\right]$, we have new $\bfx$:$\left[\begin{array}{cccc}{3} & {1} & {0} & {1}\\ {6} & {2} & {1} & {1}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{0} \\ {0} \\ {x_3} \\ {x_4}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{c}{4} \\ {5}\end{array}\right]$.
\\~\\After doing matrix algebra, we get our $\bfx_\beta=\left[\begin{array}{c}{0} \\ {0} \\ {4} \\ {1}\end{array}\right]$ with a cost of $2(0)-(0)-4=-4$.
\\~\\\textbf{(b)} To check if the solution is optimal, we have to look at the other two adjacent corners to see if we increase or decrease cost.
\\~\\Consider the basis $\beta=\{1, 2\}$: $\left[\begin{array}{cccc}{3} & {1} & {0} & {1}\\ {6} & {2} & {1} & {1}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{x_1} \\ {x_2} \\ {0} \\ {0}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{c}{4} \\ {5}\end{array}\right]$. 
\\~\\This is not a feasible solution because the matrix $\left[\begin{array}{cc}{3} & {1} \\ {6} & {2}\end{array}\right]$ has linearly dependent columns, which are the coefficients of $x_1$ and $x_2$.
